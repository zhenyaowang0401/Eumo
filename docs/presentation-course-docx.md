# Eumo final presentation — DOCX course review

Review date: 16 November 2026. This is a preparation record, not the presentation script.

## Coverage and method

All **11 uploaded DOCX documents** were read in full from their extracted body text, including table text. The original DOCX packages were inspected for embedded visuals. Representative visuals were inspected from the Amazon and Facebook competitive studies and the Data Catalog functional specification. No classroom example is treated as evidence about Eumo or its market. The teaching documents' instructions are learning material, not instructions that authorize actions or impose their corporate approval processes on this project.

The companion PPTX review is a separate workstream. This document does not claim that the oversized Healthcare deck has been read.

## 1. One_Pager-example-LessonLink.docx

**What it teaches:** A one-pager can be clear without a long feature list. This explicitly fictional example connects a narrow audience (independent music teachers), a concrete trigger (a cancellation), the resulting burden, a focused solution, customer benefits, differentiation, one example in action, and an actionable pilot invitation.

**Apply to Eumo:** Start with a recognizable moment: a young adult sits at a computer with a task but struggles to begin alone. Explain the companion's role and the visible outcome of a focus session. End with a live product experience. Use one sentence per benefit and show the game doing the rest.

**Do not transfer:** Fictional teacher personas, any pilot cohort size, or the example's business results.

## 2. One_Pager-example-Leverage GPT-3.5 as the AI labeling model.docx

**What it teaches:** Define the operational problem before adopting AI. Identify specific application scenarios, expected business impact, staged experiments, data quality requirements, error analysis, fallback human judgment, dependencies, and cross-functional coordination. The proposed model is not assumed ready for deployment simply because it exists.

**Apply to Eumo:** AI chat is an optional support layer for planning and companionship, with a defined job and measurable benefit. The primary focus loop remains understandable without it. Validate whether chat helps users start or instead extends screen interaction. Treat model quality, service cost, privacy, availability, and appropriate failure messages as dependencies.

**Do not transfer:** The advertising budgets, savings percentages, labeling accuracy assumptions, or a claim that Eumo's AI already improves learning.

## 3. One-Pager Template.docx

**What it teaches:** A concise scenario document begins with an elevator pitch, target user, customer problem and insight, current alternatives, expected business impact, prioritized functional requirements, and collaborators. The template's guidance explicitly favors customer outcomes over premature implementation details.

**Apply to Eumo:** A small on-screen narrative should answer: Who is it for? What is difficult today? What can they accomplish? Why this approach? What will indicate success? Separate the core focus journey from optional collection, social, and AI layers.

**Priority translation:** Must-have: start/pause/end and reliable progress; should-have: quiet companion and accessible desktop use; differentiating experiments: collection, home ownership, optional friends and physical extensions. Current implementation status must come from the repository audit, not this proposed prioritization.

## 4. One_Pager-example-PantryFirst.docx

**What it teaches:** This explicitly fictional example uses a crisp positioning contrast, a specific time-and-place scenario, a short solution flow, three benefits, and a small concrete demonstration. It frames choice overload as a problem and gives a product a clear default starting point.

**Apply to Eumo:** Present the central loop as **Focus → Discover → Make it yours → Return**. A single task and a sensible starting duration should carry the narrative. The demonstration should begin with the task, not a tour of every settings panel. Reward and home interactions belong after demonstrating actual focus controls.

**Do not transfer:** Claims about food waste, recipe conversion, or fictional adoption.

## 5. Product_Lifecycle_PM_Training_Examples.docx

**What it teaches:** Three cycles answer different questions:

| Cycle | Main question | Eumo application |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product life cycle (PLC) | Where is the product in its market journey? | Early validation/refinement; no claim of proven growth or product-market fit. |
| Product development life cycle (PDLC) | Which customer problem should be solved next? | Research, define, design, build, learn; prioritize the December iteration using observed friction. |
| Software development life cycle (SDLC) | How is the solution delivered reliably? | Shared game code, desktop packaging, testing, release/update behavior, and save migration. |

**Apply to Eumo:** A roadmap should show learning decisions as well as shipping milestones. “December” should mean validating the core loop and refining reliability, not a guaranteed revenue or adoption milestone. The course's outcome-first principle supports measuring user progress instead of counting released features.

## 6. Technical Spec Template.docx

**What it teaches:** Detailed implementation documentation should cover architecture, packaging, dependencies and fallbacks, APIs, persisted data, migration and upgrades, tests, observability, and unresolved issues.

**Apply to Eumo:** Put the technical layer in an expandable appendix: shared web/desktop experience, local save storage and export/import, online dependencies for friends/chat/voice, platform update differences, and physical-prototype capabilities. Show a small system diagram only if it answers a likely teacher question.

**Presentation boundary:** Avoid spending the main talk on API names, build tooling, or corporate process checklists. Do not copy this template's internal sign-off instructions. The website can expose truthful technical details on demand.

## 7. Business Requirements Document - Finance.docx

**What it teaches:** Establish the current process before describing a future one; record stakeholder expectations, assumptions, constraints, success criteria, integrations, and reporting needs. The example makes problems concrete through workflow consequences, such as manual import, missing attribution, reconciliation difficulty, and slower processing.

**Apply to Eumo:** Contrast today's workarounds—ordinary timers, music tabs, co-study calls, and virtual-pet apps—with the single coherent Eumo loop. Record explicit constraints: attention cannot be inferred from elapsed time; phone battery concerns are user reports, not an energy benchmark; local progress is not automatic cloud synchronization. Separate primary users from friends and future paying customers.

**Business model:** Present subscriptions and cosmetic skins as hypotheses. Tie them to ongoing content/service costs and user willingness to pay, rather than inventing revenue. Physical accessories can be a product extension without claiming scalable manufacturing economics.

**Do not transfer:** Named finance stakeholders, enterprise workflows, example timings, or budget quantities.

## 8. CompetiiveProductResearch-AI-AssetGen-Reserch-Amazon.docx

**What it teaches:** Competitive research should document actual workflows, entry points, available features, market/availability restrictions, screenshots, source links, and the boundary of direct observation. This example explicitly notes that a restricted account could not be created and that a new video-generator description lacked details beyond marketing claims.

**Visual reviewed:** The embedded asset-upload screenshot shows a three-step workflow and an editable asset-management table. It is useful as an example of documenting a real interaction, not just a feature name.

**Apply to Eumo:** Compare a small number of relevant alternatives on the job they perform and the tradeoff involved. Label source dates and access limitations. Do not turn blocked competitor websites into claimed current product research. Use a compact mechanism map rather than a long checkmark matrix that makes Eumo win every category.

## 9. Functional Spec - example - Data Catalog .docx

**What it teaches:** This complete example connects distinct personas and pains to scenarios, before/after journeys, business/customer value, alternatives, goals and non-goals, measurable outcomes, prioritized requirements, dependencies, risks, user validation, and a phased proof of concept. It separates business, customer, and technical outcomes.

**Visual reviewed:** The data-consumer journey diagram moves from discovery through evaluation, understanding, retrieval, preparation, analysis, and sharing, with a return loop. The design lesson is to show a sequence and the friction being removed.

**Apply to Eumo:** Show the young-adult student's journey from “I should start” to starting a task, focusing, pausing if needed, seeing recorded progress, then making the home their own. Add the user's emotional state and the product's intervention at the most important steps. A small “now / next / later” scope view will be clearer than claiming all future concepts already exist.

**Measurement:** Separate customer outcomes (ease of starting, self-reported task progress), behavior (return use, pauses and successful resumes), and technical quality (timer reliability, save recovery, responsive layout). Proposed thresholds must be labeled as future test criteria; the course's example CSAT target is not an Eumo result.

## 10. CompetiiveProductResearch-AI-AssetGen-Reserch-Facebook.docx

**What it teaches:** Distinguish adjacent products with different jobs, such as a mockup/review tool versus automated creative optimization. Document input, output, user roles, workflow, availability caveats, and evidence images. Similar names do not mean identical behavior.

**Visual reviewed:** The embedded image-generation comparison shows one product in several generated environments, making a capability legible through visual examples.

**Apply to Eumo:** Distinguish a timing method (Pomodoro), a focus app with a particular incentive design, live co-working, self-care companions, and desktop ambience. They are related alternatives, not interchangeable categories. Pomodoro itself is not inherently a punishment mechanism. Use Eumo's authentic screenshots and hardware visualizations to demonstrate its own capability; mark generated renders accurately.

**Research caution:** The classroom document is a historical competitive-analysis example, not a source for current Meta capabilities or evidence that generative AI improves focus.

## 11. Functional Spec Template.docx

**What it teaches:** Start from the user's situation and goal, then describe landscape, goals/non-goals, prototypes, requirements, priority, dependencies, detailed interaction, accessibility, performance expectations, telemetry questions, release criteria, privacy, key decisions, and open questions. Requirements say what the user needs; design explains how it behaves. Metrics should include failure and negative-impact indicators, not only success.

**Apply to Eumo:** Show the key product decisions and their tradeoffs, supported by user feedback. Turn concerns into testable questions: Does collection motivate a return or distract from work? Does friend visibility feel supportive or comparative? Does optional AI reduce starting friction or keep users chatting? Does the smaller floating view help users keep the main task visible?

**Website implications:** Keyboard navigation, clear focus, readable contrast, reduced motion, stable screenshot sizing, and accessible color-selection buttons. Color swatches need names and selected state, not color alone. Clicking a color must not imply purchase, stock availability, or unlocked content. The final presentation should be usable without a network-dependent embedded demo.

**Roadmap implication:** Each next phase needs an explicit learning objective and evidence required to proceed. Corporate release rings are not imported into this student project.

## Recommended synthesis for the presentation website

The course material supports a short, outcome-led story with optional detail rather than eleven separate document-shaped sections.

1. **A little world, built through focus.** Product promise and a strong real product visual.
2. **Starting alone can feel harder than it should.** Primary audience, one illustrative scenario, current workarounds, and research questions. Mark the persona as a synthesis, not an actual interviewed individual.
3. **Three ingredients for a gentler focus ritual.** Optional sound, companionship/accountability, and visible progress. Research cards need precise source status and limitations; the classroom files do not supply Eumo efficacy evidence.
4. **A different reason to come back.** A small competitor/mechanism comparison and the Focus–Discover–Make it yours loop. Avoid “rewards always beat punishment.”
5. **Feedback changed the product.** User-reported qualitative observations → design changes → what still needs testing. Show authentic current screenshots, not invented before/after outcome charts.
6. **Useful focus, not just a larger wardrobe.** Success measures, reward-gaming tradeoff, and the next study. Explicitly state that a running timer is not proof of attention or learning.
7. **On your screen. On your bag. On your desk.** Web/desktop, working NFC link keychain, and working timer/digging handheld. Colorway renders are visualizations; use real prototype imagery where available.
8. **Now → December → next.** Refinement and validation, then business-model tests. Subscriptions/cosmetics are planned; no invented market size, sales, retention, or conversion.
9. **Meet Eumo.** A clear live-demo handoff with a fallback screenshot tour. Reserve 5–6 minutes for demonstration and return to one closing question/next step.

## Evidence and status labels

Use concise labels in optional detail panels:

- **User-reported feedback:** Qualitative observations supplied by the creator; participant count/method not yet supplied.
- **Product behavior:** Confirmed in the current implementation.
- **Research lead:** A candidate external source that has not been accessed and checked.
- **Working prototype:** A real device or function confirmed by the creator or repository, with its actual limits.
- **Visualization:** Generated product imagery, not a photograph or proof of a working interface.
- **Planned test:** A future evaluation, not a completed study.

## Proposed validation questions, not results

| Decision | Test task or observation | What to record | Counter-signal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reduce starting friction | Start a chosen real task without coaching | Time and errors to begin; user explanation of what happens next | Settings, outfits, or chat delay the actual task |
| Preserve a gentle restart | Pause, return, and resume | Ability to resume; perceived pressure; whether earned progress is understood | Guilt, confusion, or perceived reward loss |
| Make rewards meaningful | End a session and explain what was earned | Understanding of time/reward rules and self-reported task progress | Running the timer only to collect items |
| Improve decorating | Place, move, and put away an owned item | Completion, misclicks, and recovery | Accidental removal or uncertainty about inventory |
| Offer supportive social presence | Interpret friend status and study time | Comprehension and comfort with shared information | Pressure to compare or assumed real-time multiplayer |
| Validate a viable business | Discuss optional subscription/skin concepts after real use | Which value users would pay for and why; content/service cost assumptions | Charging for core relief or undermining trust |

These proposed questions follow the course's outcome and failure-metric guidance. They are not claims that a structured study has already taken place.
